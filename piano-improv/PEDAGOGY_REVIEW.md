# Interlude: teaching-content review

Reviewed on 25 October 2026. This is a source-based review by the app's AI developer. It is not a review or endorsement by a piano teacher, the authors below, or their publishers. Browser tests cannot establish teaching effectiveness.

## Material actually consulted

1. **Forrest Kinney, Create First! and Pattern Play** — the author's detailed [duet-to-solo teaching guide](https://forrestkinney.com/improvising) and [How It Works](https://forrestkinney.com/how-it-works), including descriptions of official demonstration lessons and sample pages. Used to check cognitive load, supported playing before solo playing, learning one element at a time, and exploration without forced outcomes. Full commercial books were not read or copied.
2. **Tim Richards, Improvising Blues Piano (Schott)** — the publisher's indexed contents and its [licensed interactive edition and course outline](https://www.musicgurus.com/sheet-music/improvising-blues-piano). The available outline starts with triads/walking bass, then riffs/rhythms and further voicings. Used to check broad sequencing, not to claim an exercise-by-exercise review of the full book. The publisher's main product page could not be fetched directly; its indexed contents and the licensed partner outline were used. No paid lessons were accessed or copied.
3. **Open Music Theory, Version 2** — full accessible chapters: [Embellishing Tones](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/embellishing-tones/), [Swing Rhythms](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/swing-rhythms/), and [Blues Harmony](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/blues-harmony/). Used to check definitions and examples of passing/neighbor motion, straight versus swung eighths, syncopation, and twelve-bar form. Explanations and examples in the app are newly written, not copied textbook content.
4. **Matt Glaser, Berklee: Five Levels** — the [published teaching article](https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-today/summer-2011/five-levels-of-improvisation), previously consulted. Used to check melody-based variation and gradually loosening constraints. This is an article, not a full review of the book from which it was adapted.

## Findings and changes

| Review finding                                                                                     | Change                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A reading-first screen could make theory a condition for music making.                             | Put A/B listening before explanations. Offer a direct route to playing. Reading and the concept check are optional.                                                                                                                                |
| Early jazz drills used E-flat against a major-third chord before explaining that tension.          | Start the shared phrase/motif lessons with simple major-chord material. In the suggested path, introduce chord tones before the dedicated blues studies. The direct blues collection remains available, with its pitches and C7 context explained. |
| A fixed timer could change steps in the middle of a phrase or before a twelve-bar chorus finishes. | Treat times as suggestions. Steps remain active until the player chooses Next. Actual active practice time is recorded.                                                                                                                            |
| Two-hand tasks could ask for bass, melody, and new harmony at once.                                | Practice the bass alone first, add one right-hand note, then a small phrase. Keep the left-hand pattern familiar while varying the melody.                                                                                                         |
| A form exercise added new blues-scale pitches while asking for a complete miniature.               | Keep its familiar four-note palette. Label extra colors in the later chorus lesson as optional rather than another requirement.                                                                                                                    |
| Transfer tasks could remove backing, note hints, and the familiar melody together.                 | Remove one support at a time. Keep backing available; independent accompaniment is a later option.                                                                                                                                                 |
| Some A/B examples changed more than the feature they claimed to isolate.                           | Keep pitches and note lengths the same in the phrase-spacing comparison. Establish C major before the tonic comparison, and change only its final melody note. The swing comparison keeps pitches and written rhythm identical.                    |
| Continuous notes or a busy accompaniment could be implied to be inherently bad.                    | Describe them as valid choices that ask for different listening or coordination, not errors.                                                                                                                                                       |
| A final G7 turnaround could be mistaken for a settled ending.                                      | Explain the return to the next C7, and distinguish finishing there from starting another chorus.                                                                                                                                                   |

## Theory checks

- C major: C–E–G. C7: C–E–G–B-flat. F7: F–A–C–E-flat.
- C–D–E illustrates passing motion; C–D–C illustrates neighbor motion. Function still depends on harmonic and rhythmic context.
- The tonic is the center of a key; it is not automatically the root of whichever chord is sounding.
- Swing playback uses a simplified 2:1 model. Real swing ratios vary; not all jazz swings.
- Syncopation and swing are different. The Joplin extract retains straight subdivisions.
- The twelve-bar backing has four beats per bar. Its final G7 is a common turnaround variant.
- Source phrases, reductions, teaching variations, and original blues studies are identified separately. These are not transcriptions of modern recordings.

## Limits and next validation

The app does not hear or judge the acoustic piano. A saved visit is not evidence of mastery. Text checks and code tests verify consistency and behavior, not how well a person will learn.

The next useful validation is a session with a qualified piano teacher and the intended learner: watch for unclear words, excessive reading, awkward ranges, loss of pulse, or tasks that remove too much support. Revise from that observation before treating the path as a complete course.
